Periodic Signals (Continuous-Time) – Concepts, Conditions & GATE Tricks

Periodic signals are fundamental in signal processing and are frequently tested in GATE ECE. A continuous-time signal is called periodic if it repeats itself after a fixed time interval. Understanding periodicity is important for analyzing continuous-time signals using the Fourier Transform and Laplace Transform.

In this article, we cover the conditions for periodicity of continuous-time signals, fundamental period, periodicity of complex exponentials, and important GATE shortcuts. These concepts also help in understanding the frequency-domain representation of periodic signals and their relationship with Fourier Transform and Laplace Transform.

Fourier Transform and Laplace Transform

The Fourier Transform represents a continuous-time signal in the frequency domain, while the Laplace Transform provides a more general representation in the complex-frequency domain. Depending on the properties and nature of the signal, these transforms can simplify signal and system analysis and make GATE-level problems easier and faster to solve.


Fourier Transform

The Fourier Transform is a powerful mathematical tool used to represent a continuous-time signal in the frequency domain. It decomposes a signal into its frequency components, allowing us to analyze the magnitude, phase, bandwidth, and spectral characteristics of the signal. The Fourier Transform is widely used in signal processing and is an important topic for GATE ECE.

Fourier Transform Forms

Fourier Transform:

$X(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt$


Inverse Fourier Transform:

$x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega$

Key Characteristics

  • The Fourier Transform represents a continuous-time signal in the frequency domain.
  • The transform $X(\omega)$ describes the frequency components present in the signal $x(t)$.
  • The inverse Fourier Transform reconstructs the original time-domain signal from its frequency-domain representation.
  • Fourier Transform properties such as linearity, time shifting, frequency shifting, scaling, differentiation, and convolution simplify GATE-level problems.
  • For periodic signals, the Fourier Transform is represented using impulses at the harmonic frequencies.
  • Choosing the appropriate Fourier Transform property can significantly reduce the calculation required in GATE problems.

Laplace Transform

The Laplace Transform is a powerful mathematical tool used to represent continuous-time signals in the complex-frequency domain. It is widely used for analyzing signals and systems, solving differential equations, and studying system behavior, stability, poles, zeros, and transient and steady-state responses. The Laplace Transform is an important topic for GATE ECE.

Laplace Transform:

Laplace Transform:

$X(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$


Inverse Laplace Transform:

$x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi j}\int_{\gamma-j\infty}^{\gamma+j\infty}X(s)e^{st}\,ds$


Key Characteristics

  • The Laplace Transform represents a continuous-time signal in the complex-frequency domain.
  • The transform $X(s)$ describes the signal in terms of the complex variable $s=\sigma+j\omega$.
  • The inverse Laplace Transform reconstructs the original time-domain signal from its Laplace-domain representation.
  • The Region of Convergence (ROC) specifies the values of $s$ for which the Laplace Transform exists.
  • Poles and zeros provide important information about the behavior and stability of continuous-time systems.
  • Properties such as linearity, time shifting, differentiation, integration, and convolution simplify GATE-level problems.
  • Choosing the appropriate Laplace Transform property can significantly reduce the calculation required in GATE problems.
